# T16b: public references for successive estimator layers

## 1. Result and reading rules

**Recommend NASA's NACA TN 1270 tapered-wing experiment as the first candidate to prepare, conditionally, after the geometry and independent section-input gaps below are closed.** It separates experimental results from its own calculations, provides lift and pitch information, and avoids a tail, fuselage and propulsion. It is not ready for an unqualified comparison today. The simpler rectangular-wing alternative has an explicitly unremoved support contribution. None of the inspected candidates clears every criterion without a qualification. (T §§Wings/Methods, pp. 4-6, Tables I-II, Fig. 4; R pp. 2-4; §8 below.)

For layer B, TN 1378 is the better follow-up lead, but its mounting body, missing stated Mach and incomplete mounting geometry prevent accepting it as a clean wing-plus-tail reference. Report 678 has additional geometry and tunnel-correction obstacles. For layer C, **RM SL56H06 cannot support the requested low-Mach comparison: its measurements are supersonic. CRM-HL / ONERA Case 2.2 is rejected for this search**, because it has deployed slats and requires component physics absent from the current estimator. (B pp. 4-7, Figs. 1-4, 8; S pp. 1-4; F pp. 1-5; O §6.4 p. 3; §6 below.)

This is a source audit dated 2026-11-20, not a modeling authorization or an accuracy verdict. No coefficient table, geometry fixture, solver, external-program run or numerical comparison is supplied. Recommendations do not select a replacement aircraft, decide D8/D9 or start T17/T10/T11. The earlier T16 proposal remains a historical record; this narrower audit supersedes its CRM-HL recommendation. (TASKS §T16 and §T16b; PLAN opening scope and §T23; T16_AUDIT §3; this audit §§6-9.)

Pages mean **printed pages**. Figure-only sheets use their printed figure number and a PDF page position. Unpaginated sources use named sections or symbols. **Partial** means useful evidence with a specified gap; **unverified** is not a pass. “Missing” means not established in the cited inspected sections, not proof that no archive contains it. Source facts, proposed future work and deductions are distinguished below. (Audit procedure; PLAN §T16 audit contract.)

## 2. What a matched comparison would mean

Current VLM represents one symmetric planar wing in incompressible, attached, small-angle flow. It requires the ideal thin-airfoil section slope, uses a flat wake, and has no thickness, profile drag or intrinsic section pitch moment. LLT accepts independently specified section lift slope and zero-lift angle, but supplies no pitch moment and is restricted to its straight-wing assumptions. A tail, body or slat cannot be made part of that calculation by relabeling its wing result. (VLM `ASSUMPTIONS`/`solve`; LLT `ASSUMPTIONS`/`solve`; PLAN §Static prediction budget.)

For layer A, compare the same wing's lift slope and the **neutral point derived from its lift and pitching-moment slopes about the stated origin**. The lift and moment must use the same area, reference chord, angle units, fixed configuration and slope interval. A center of pressure at one lift, a local section aerodynamic center, and a whole-wing neutral point are not interchangeable. A neutral point reconstructed from the same measured slopes is useful but is not an independent third measurement. (C §3.1 pp. 25-28; R p. 2; T Symbols pp. 2-4; W Part II Notation pp. 40-41; PLAN §T24.)

For layer B, retain the actual tail incidence and position and compare matched tail-on/tail-off lift and pitch curves before deriving a total neutral point. An effective downwash inferred through a tail-response model is a different observable from a measured local flow angle. Mounting-body loads cannot be assumed to cancel in a tail-on minus tail-off difference. (B pp. 3, 5-7; S pp. 1-4; C §§2.6/3.1; PLAN §§T19/T21.)

For layer C, geometry/reference matching alone does not provide a supersonic or whole-aircraft model. T17 addresses independently sourced section laws; T18 adds orientations/vector loads; T19 adds wing-tail coupling; T20/T21 address body loads/interference. T22's proposed subsonic PG treatment would not turn an incompressible calculation into a supersonic one. All are future, separately authorized work. (PLAN §§T17-T22; A §§Configurations/Compressibility; F pp. 1-5.)

## 8. Proposed first comparison and interim code cross-checks

### First experimental comparison, conditional on closing its gates

Use T's smooth Fig. 4 wing at its documented Reynolds condition, with the exact graph-supplied Mach left unverified until resolved. Reconstruct its actual planform including the rounded tip, sourced twist/section variation and published reference area/MAC. Use the **experimental column/curve only** for lift slope; report a local fixed-configuration derivative with an agreed extraction interval and degree-to-radian conversion. Later pitch/neutral-point work must use that same geometry, condition and origin; it needs separate approval/checkpoint under PLAN. (T pp. 3-6, Tables I-II, Figs. 1/3/4; PLAN §§T17/T23/T24.)

The estimator pieces would be planar VLM plus independently sourced section inputs after T17; LLT can provide a separate straight-wing lift calculation. Missing pieces remain the geometry prescriptions, independent section laws and precise condition evidence already listed. Thickness/viscous effects and tunnel-correction uncertainty must be reported as omissions; T17 is not a full viscous solver. No measured coefficient is an input used to tune the prediction, and no acceptable-error threshold is proposed. Until those gates close, the verdict is **prepare this candidate; do not start the experimental comparison**. (T Methods/Calculations pp. 5-6; VLM/LLT `ASSUMPTIONS`; PLAN §§T17/T23; §3 A1 above.)

### What AVL and OpenVSP could cross-check meanwhile

| Quantity | Proposed cross-check and limitation |
|---|---|
| Geometry and references | Compare derived area, span, MAC/reference origin and the actual discretized planar surface. Convert AVL's downstream-positive x/up-positive z to project axes. Match rounded tips, twist/zero-lift definition and section idealization before comparing outputs. (A §§Coordinate system/Geometry Input File/CLAF; VLM `VlmRequest`; T Fig. 1.) |
| Wing lift/pitch slopes and neutral point | At matched incompressible, attached, small-angle conditions, compare CL, Cm, their alpha derivatives and the neutral point derived about a common origin. Set compatible section laws; do not use an external code's extra camber/thickness/body capability silently. This is possible as an idealized wing cross-check before experimental inputs are ready. (A §§Configurations/Output/Stability derivatives/CLAF; V §§Introduction/Reference Values; VLM `VlmOutput`; C §3.1 pp. 25-28.) |
| Induced drag and span loading | Compare induced drag using the stated wake/force method and spanwise loading, with mesh convergence evidence. Do not compare VLM's induced drag to experimental total drag, or assume two numerical methods must agree at finite resolution. (A §§General Description/Output; V §Results Manager; VLM `drag_method`; T pp. 5-6.) |
| Wing-tail increments, later | After T18/T19, compare component and total lift/pitch derivatives, tail loading and changes with tail position/incidence using matched body omissions. Current wing-only output cannot supply this check today. (A §§Configurations/Stability derivatives; PLAN §§T18/T19; B Fig. 12.) |

These are **cross-checking, not validation**: agreement between programs does not show agreement with experiment or establish that omitted effects are small. Pin the actual program release and settings, independently refine meshes, state wake/section/reference differences and review artifact licences before committing external outputs. No AVL/OpenVSP run is authorized or performed by this audit. NASA's current VSPAERO page warns that its older tutorials predate solver changes, so a future cross-check must use documentation matching its actual release. (N §Introduction; A §§Configurations/Compressibility; V §§Disclaimer/GUI Overview; PLAN §Later AVL cross-check and artifact policy.)
