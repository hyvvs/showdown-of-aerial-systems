# Estimator plan before a published Navion derivative comparison

**Recommendation:** first make a sourced, control-fixed comparison of whole-aircraft lift slope, then pitching-moment slope. Build the common reference/axis handling, section data, non-planar surfaces, coupled tails/downwash and body/interference contributions before claiming either result represents the Navion. Assess finite-Mach and propulsion effects before accepting the comparison. Add controls, rates, drag and unsteady terms as separate milestones; they are not prerequisites to every static-slope comparison. This is a proposed order, not authorization to implement T16 onward or to choose a validation tolerance.

The reason is a mismatch of objects: the current estimator predicts an isolated idealized wing; CR-96008 publishes Navion aircraft derivatives without a component breakdown. A wing result cannot be relabeled an aircraft result. The unresolved geometry, reference area, axes and propulsion-state issues in the [decision sheet](../data/reference/inventory/go_no_go.md) and [CR inventory](../data/reference/inventory/cr96008.md) remain data gates, not quantities the estimator should invent (CR pp. 100-103, A-1-A-7, B-1; current implementation references below).

## Scope, notation and source record

Queued [T10](TASKS.md#t10-compare-against-reference-needs-h3-and-d9) still means power-off stall, glide, trim and static-margin comparisons. The derivative comparison requested here would be a separately agreed milestone supporting that work; this plan does not rewrite T10 or complete H3/H4, D8/D9 or T11. Derivative agreement alone would not establish a stall model, total drag polar or power-off trim case (TASKS §§H3/H4/T10/T11; CR pp. 101-103).

A derivative describes how a force or moment coefficient changes when an angle, control, speed parameter or rotation rate changes. `CL`, `CD`, `CY` mean lift, drag and side-force coefficients; `Cm`, `Cl`, `Cn` mean pitch, roll and yaw coefficients. `alpha` is angle of attack, `beta` sideslip, `p/q/r` body roll/pitch/yaw rates, and `delta_e/a/r` elevator/aileron/rudder deflections. **Mach `M` is not pitching moment.** Rate normalization and source axes are part of each derivative's identity (CR A-2-A-7; C §§1.2, 4.3).

## What would be compared

The minimum proposed published target is `CL_alpha`, followed by `Cm_alpha`, at the source's condition and with controls fixed. A broader stage could compare the thirteen derivative fields represented by the current simulator: `CL_alpha`, `Cm_alpha`, `Cm_delta_e`, `CY_beta`, `Cl_beta`, `Cn_beta`, `Cl_delta_a`, `Cn_delta_r`, `Cm_q`, `Cl_p`, `Cn_r`, `Cl_r`, `Cn_p`. The simulator's ability to **consume** these fields is not an estimator prediction capability (CR pp. 101-102; AIRCRAFT_FORMAT §§“Coefficient fields”, “Dynamic derivatives and limits”).

The matrices below list every nondimensional derivative in X-B/C, plus `Cm_delta_e`, which can be recovered from the dimensional `M_delta_e` definition after the references are resolved. Thus the owner can select a smaller first stage without silently losing the other published targets. `CL_q` is not a printed X-B row. Dimensional X-D/E quantities and the transfer-function factors on p. 103 are not additional independent aerodynamic truths: later comparisons would also involve the reference state, mass/inertia and equations defining them; X-E's primed terms include inertia coupling. X-D/E have no axes note on their own page, so their frame remains a provenance check before conversion (CR pp. 101-103, A-8-A-10).

**Important qualification for every row:** CR does not say how much of the aircraft derivative comes from each component, or establish the power/propeller condition. “Contributions” below means the physical budget a prediction must assess, **not a measured decomposition**. Wing, tail and body loads interact: downwash/sidewash is a change in flow at a surface, not an extra load to add twice. Every row also needs a finite-Mach assessment and an unresolved propulsion assessment: propeller slipstream, propeller forces/drag and their changes may matter, but their inclusion in these compiled values cannot be determined. Neither effect is currently produced by the estimator; either model it with sourced inputs or justify its omission against the agreed scope/error budget. Do not infer an engine-off condition from the report's silence (CR pp. 100-103; C §§2.5-2.7, 4.3; J §4; D §2.2.1; P §11.7).

In the tables, **interaction** includes wing/body interference and coupled surface response. “None” means no current implemented estimator derivative, not a physical zero. All rows require T16's comparison contract and T28's final evidence checks; finite-Mach work is T22 and conditional propulsion work T27.

## Proposed build order: T16 onward

These are proposals in this document, **not new active entries in TASKS.md**. T22's compressibility assessment precedes control/rate implementation so it need not delay the first static comparison behind those modules. T28 is a comparison checkpoint used when each target's prerequisites are ready, not a requirement to finish every later model first. Sizes are relative planning judgments: small = focused adapter/evidence work; medium = one bounded model/interface; large = coupled geometry/solver or several inseparable physical effects. They are not time estimates.

All future tests below would have their expected symbolic result and independently calculated synthetic case written **before** implementation. No test values, tolerances, panel settings or mutation results are supplied here. Refinement, finite differences of the same solver and comparisons with AVL are useful secondary checks; none replaces an independent analytic/hand expectation. Numerical verification does not establish Navion validity. Every deliberate mutation listed below must fail its intended test before the associated evidence is accepted (AGENTS Batch mode; existing [VLM](../data/reference/estimator/vlm.md) and [lifting-line](../data/reference/estimator/lifting_line.md) verification records).

### T16 — Agree and verify the comparison contract — medium

- **Purpose/contribution:** establish what will be differentiated, at which source state, in which units/axes, about which origin, with which controls/propulsion held fixed. Resolve or quarantine the Navion area conflict and blank lateral trim-angle cell; obtain variant, geometry, section data, common CG/reference datum and physical control mapping. Do not silently fill either cell from another source. This adds trustworthy transformations, not aerodynamic physics (CR pp. 100-102, A-1-A-10, B-1; [sign audit](SIGN_EVIDENCE.md)).
- **Method/source:** express forces/moments as vectors, transfer the moment origin, and differentiate the complete transformation. Fixed source stability axes differ from the project's instantaneous force axes; derivatives of rotations can introduce terms involving the baseline force. Match angle/rate normalization. Recover `Cm_delta_e` from the dimensional definition only after checking pitch inertia and reference quantities (CR A-1, A-6-A-10, B-1; C §4.3 pp. 56-58; J §6.3).
- **Independent tests:** rotate a prescribed constant force by a symbolic angle and differentiate by hand; shift a prescribed force/moment between distinct origins using a cross product; manually convert an angular and a normalized-rate derivative; normalize a prescribed dimensional elevator moment both ways. The prescribed loads are synthetic, not generated by the estimator.
- **Mutations:** reverse a rotation, omit its derivative/baseline-force term, use the opposite reference-arm sign, confuse degree/radian or span/chord normalization, or relabel a paired deflection as a single-surface angle.
- **Risks / use:** missing baseline/reference information may prevent a unique conversion. No guessed datum or propulsion state. Required for every Navion or F-104 published comparison; a documented unresolved input stops only the dependent comparison.

### T17 — Sourced section lift and moment inputs — medium

- **Purpose/contribution:** replace the VLM's mandatory ideal section slope where justified by independent section data; retain zero-lift angle, incidence and section pitching-moment law with provenance. This is wing/tail section physics, not a fit to Navion aircraft slopes. LLT already accepts section slope/zero-lift inputs; current VLM rejects slopes outside its ideal assumption (repository references; A `CLAF`; C §2.3-2.4).
- **Method/source:** a linear section lift law with a checked boundary-condition treatment, such as A's `CLAF`-style control-point adjustment, plus independently supplied section-moment dependence where known. The primer describes the slope mechanism but the detailed mapping and its effect on moments must be derived/verified before coding. Do not count the same section moment in both the lattice loading and an added moment. Do not scale final aircraft coefficients to match the target. Preserve the existing ideal-section mode and its placement assumptions (A `CLAF`; C §2.4 pp. 18-19; J §§4.5, 5.1-5.2).
- **Independent tests:** prescribed linear section lift/zero-lift/moment laws integrated over a rectangular strip; the analytic elliptic LLT slope with independently specified section slope; high-span two-dimensional limiting behavior. Cross-reference existing ideal-wing hand cases without changing their expectations.
- **Mutations:** ignore section slope, reverse zero-lift angle/incidence, drop the section moment or apply section area normalization twice.
- **Risks / use:** section data, Reynolds dependence and camber may be missing; potential-flow thickness correction is not a viscous model. Needed to match Navion wing/tail assumptions, and similarly needed for F-104; independent inputs remain a source gate.

### T18 — Arbitrary surface orientation and full vector loads — large

- **Purpose/contribution:** permit non-planar wing geometry and independently placed horizontal/vertical surfaces, sideslip, unsymmetrical controls later, and full force/moment vectors about an explicit datum. Existing planar/symmetry restrictions cannot simply be bypassed (repository VLM/planform; J §§2.1, 3.1, 4.1-4.2, 6.1).
- **Method/source:** horseshoe-vortex influence projected onto actual panel normals, with vector Kutta-Joukowski loads and reference-arm moments. Convert source geometry conventions explicitly; trailing wakes follow downstream flow, which is along body negative x in the existing linearized convention. Keep the small-angle/attached-flow scope (J cited sections; existing [vortex convention](../data/reference/estimator/vortex.md)).
- **Independent tests:** rigidly rotate a hand-solved isolated surface, all vortex endpoints and its freestream and recover the rotated load; rotate a horizontal lifting surface into a vertical one and check side force/moments; manually evaluate an off-axis horseshoe and cross-product moment; zero-dihedral mode recovers the existing planar hand solution.
- **Mutations:** keep a global vertical normal, discard side force or a moment-arm component, mirror the fin incorrectly, or send wakes upstream.
- **Risks / use:** intersecting surfaces/wakes, near-singular geometry and panel orientation need explicit diagnostics, not an invented core radius. Both aircraft need this for complete lateral/tail comparisons; the first longitudinal target only uses the relevant wing/horizontal-tail subset.

### T19 — Coupled wing and tails, including downwash/sidewash — large

- **Purpose/contribution:** solve the wing, horizontal tail and vertical tail together. Their influence must enter the same tangency problem; summing independent lifting-surface coefficients misses wake interaction (C §2.6 p. 20, §4.3 pp. 60-61; J §§4.2, 5.2-5.3).
- **Method/source:** include the velocities induced by every lifting surface and its wake at every other surface; integrate loads separately and as an aircraft total. Obtain tail flow from the modeled wake, not an adjustable downwash factor fitted to the Navion. A flat prescribed wake is the initial approximation, not a roll-up/separation model (J cited sections; A “Configurations”).
- **Independent tests:** solve a deliberately small wing/tail linear system by hand using independently evaluated vortex influences; compare a prescribed wake's induced velocity at an isolated tail with a direct hand Biot-Savart integral; check zero coupling and removed-tail limits. A scalar prescribed-downwash tail-lift/lever-arm calculation tests response, without treating that approximation as an exact oracle for the full lattice.
- **Mutations:** omit cross-surface influence, reverse downwash, discard the tail arm, normalize each tail by its own area when reporting aircraft coefficients, or omit the vertical-tail wake.
- **Risks / use:** wake placement near tails, tail dynamic-pressure loss and viscous wakes may exceed this model. Needed for Navion `CL_alpha`/`Cm_alpha` and subsequent tail/control/lateral targets; also needed for F-104, with additional regime limitations.

### T20 — Isolated fuselage contribution — medium

- **Purpose/contribution:** add body loads and destabilizing pitch/yaw stiffness separately from lifting surfaces, with a checkable isolated-body baseline (C §§2.5, 4.3 pp. 19-20, 60-61; J §§2.2, 3.2, 4.3, 6.1.3).
- **Method/source:** slender-body source/transverse-doublet representation from a sourced centerline and cross-section distribution; initially verify a smooth closed body of revolution. C's closed-body volume result is a useful analytic limit, not a complete model of a real cabin/fuselage or wing-body interference. A explicitly cautions about limited experience with slender-body modeling.
- **Independent tests:** integrate an analytic closed-body area distribution by hand: opposite end loads cancel in the stated ideal limit, while its pitch couple has the source-derived volume dependence and is independent of moment origin. Rotate the symmetric body for the corresponding yaw case; shrinking body cross-section removes its load contribution.
- **Mutations:** replace area by width, reverse the streamwise derivative, lose an end contribution, reverse the pitch/yaw sign or apply a CG shift to a pure couple incorrectly.
- **Risks / use:** noncircular/blunt geometry, separation and crossflow are limited or absent. Required assessment for Navion whole-aircraft pitch/directional derivatives; its lift contribution may vanish only in the tested ideal closed-body limit, not by assumption for the real aircraft. F-104 also needs body effects.

### T21 — Wing-body interference without double counting — large

- **Purpose/contribution:** capture body-induced flow at the wing/tails and lifting-surface-induced changes in body loads. Adding isolated wing/body numbers does not do this (C §2.5 p. 19; J §4.3 pp. 9-10, §6.1.3 p. 21).
- **Method/source:** start with J's explicitly approximate treatment: body source/doublet strengths use freestream and rotation, their field enters surface velocities, and surface-induced flow enters body load evaluation. This is **not fully coupled body-strength determination**; J states that omission. Do not describe it as exact wing-body coupling. A junction/viscous empirical correction needs a separately verified method/data set; DATCOM's underlying correlations and Nelson's example have not been verified here (J p. 10; D pp. 8-9; C p. 19).
- **Independent tests:** hand-evaluate a simple body's source/doublet velocity at a lifting element and that element's velocity at a body station; use those prescribed fields to hand-compute load changes. Removing body strength or the surface must recover the respective isolated case. Check the total moment against a separate force/arm sum and retain a ledger distinguishing isolated and interaction loads.
- **Mutations:** disable either interaction direction, add isolated-body force twice, omit wing-root surface geometry or reverse the body-induced velocity.
- **Risks / use:** root/body intersections, slender-body approximations and viscous junction effects remain. Both aircraft require an assessment; this task supplies an inviscid approximation, not proof that the Navion interference error is small.

### T22 — Linear subsonic compressibility and Mach sensitivity — medium

- **Purpose/contribution:** distinguish an incompressible prediction from a finite-Mach aircraft/section prediction; support the printed Mach derivatives where their definition permits it. This is not exclusively F-104 work (CR p. 101; C §4.3 pp. 56-57; A “Compressibility”).
- **Method/source:** Prandtl-Glauert transformation of the linear potential-flow problem, with consistent geometry, boundary-condition and load recovery. Do not multiply every three-dimensional coefficient by a two-dimensional lift correction. A verifies the method family/limits; the exact three-dimensional mapping for our conventions is **not derived or verified here** and must be written and checked before implementation. Section Reynolds/viscous Mach dependence is additional input/model work, not supplied by PG (A “Compressibility”, `CLAF`, `CDCL`).
- **Independent tests:** the zero-Mach limit recovers the incompressible hand case; the high-span two-dimensional limit follows the analytic PG lift/pressure relation; hand-differentiate that relation for Mach sensitivity. A deliberately simple transformed-geometry hand problem must check coordinates, normals and force recovery separately. Reject unsupported regimes rather than continue through a singular transformation.
- **Mutations:** reverse the compressibility factor, apply it twice, transform forces but not geometry, omit the derivative of the transformation or accept a supersonic input in the subsonic method.
- **Risks / use:** PG is not a transonic/shock, separated-flow, aeroelastic or supersonic model. Navion's low-subsonic static comparison might justify an incompressible approximation only through an agreed, source-backed error budget; it is not an exact finite-Mach match, and a Mach-derivative target cannot be established by assuming Mach independence. F-104's suitable subsonic subset also needs assessment; PG does not unlock its supersonic tables.

### T23 — Control-surface effectiveness — medium

- **Purpose/contribution:** elevator/rudder/independent left-right aileron load changes and full moment effectiveness. Keep physical travel, paired command, trim offset and source control definitions separate (CR A-4; SIGN_EVIDENCE tables).
- **Method/source:** hinged thin-surface boundary-condition/normal changes with an explicit hinge line and spanwise control extent; solve the coupled aircraft response. Use thin-airfoil plain-flap theory as an independent limiting benchmark, not a guessed efficiency factor (C §2.7 pp. 20-22; J §5.1 pp. 11-12; A `CONTROL`).
- **Independent tests:** integrate the prescribed thin-airfoil flap loading independently; vanishing flap chord removes effectiveness, while whole-chord rotation recovers the incidence response in the matching ideal limit. A prescribed tail/fin side or lift force gives exact lever-arm moment ratios. Symmetric elevator and differential aileron cases must obey D6 after mapping; magnitude tests must detect normalization errors, not only signs.
- **Mutations:** reverse each surface sign separately, use full-difference where half-difference was intended, move the hinge to the wrong end, reverse differential mirroring or use span instead of chord for pitch moment.
- **Risks / use:** hinge gaps, viscous separation, nonlinear travel and aeroelasticity are not thin-surface predictions. Needed for the Navion control rows, not for a control-fixed `CL_alpha`-only first milestone. F-104 additionally needs a verified stabilizer/aileron/rudder mechanism match; a yaw-damper derivative is not interchangeable with rudder effectiveness (other-source inventory, MEMO 2-2-59H pp. 3-5, 36). Adverse-yaw/drag comparisons also need T25.

### T24 — Quasi-steady rotation-rate derivatives — large

- **Purpose/contribution:** local inflow changes from body `p/q/r`, producing `Cm_q`, `Cl_p`, `Cn_r`, `Cl_r`, `Cn_p` for the coupled configuration. These are distinct from alpha-dot and beta-dot terms (J §4.1 p. 8, §7 pp. 27-28; C §4.3 pp. 58-69).
- **Method/source:** apply the relative-flow rotation term about the agreed rate origin at each load/control location; differentiate the coupled load solution and normalize rates by the source's chord/span and reference speed. J's velocity decomposition and sensitivity equations give the approach; A's “Unsteady flow” limits it to quasi-steady, slow motions, with no unsteady shedding.
- **Independent tests:** hand-solve a small rotating-surface system with independently calculated vortex influences, local rotation velocities and vector forces. Also integrate a prescribed high-span linear strip load under roll and calculate isolated tail pitch/fin yaw damping from local inflow and lever arms. Strip-law results test their stated model/limit, not exact finite-wing VLM values. Their ideal direct-damping moments oppose the rate and have the hand-derived arm dependence. Use nonzero offsets and unequal reference lengths to test cross derivatives and normalization; rederive C's ambiguous normalization noted above.
- **Mutations:** reverse the rotation cross product, evaluate all flow at CG, omit speed variation along a rotating surface, swap rate/reference lengths or return a combined damping derivative in place of an individual one.
- **Risks / use:** a damping sign is not universal for arbitrary configurations; only the specified analytic cases carry that expectation. Frozen-wake/quasi-steady errors need T26's assessment. Required for the Navion rate rows and later dynamic simulator comparisons, not first static slopes. Both aircraft need such work.

### T25 — Total drag and drag-dependent derivatives — large

- **Purpose/contribution:** add independently sourced section/profile, body/appendage and junction drag to coupled induced drag; support `CD_alpha`, `CD_delta_e`, `CD_M` and drag-sensitive yaw derivatives. Current `CDi` is not total `CD` (repository VLM assumptions/output; J §§6.1.5, 6.2.5; A `CDCL`).
- **Method/source:** integrate explicitly supplied section polars and a separate body/interference drag model, with Mach/Reynolds/control indexing where supported. J supplies viscous load integration and A a section-polar interface; neither is Navion drag data. A Navion body/junction correlation and off/windmilling propeller drag are **not verified**; no default drag factor or total-aircraft calibration is proposed (CR pp. 100-103; D §2.2.1).
- **Independent tests:** hand-integrate a prescribed analytic section polar over a simple wing at prescribed loading; differentiate it analytically with respect to angle/control/Mach; add a known synthetic body drag load and check its moment. Compare differential left/right drag moments with a hand cross-product sum. A zero supplied profile term must recover induced-only accounting.
- **Mutations:** omit profile drag, count junction drag twice, use total `CD` as `CD0`, mix local/global reference areas or differentiate a polar while incorrectly holding its lift argument fixed.
- **Risks / use:** transition, Reynolds dependence, separation and control drag require data beyond inviscid VLM. Needed for the Navion drag/adverse-yaw targets and eventually glide; not all are required for a first lift-slope comparison. F-104 additionally needs regime-specific wave/base/inlet drag treatment, not this subsonic build-up alone.

### T26 — Separate unsteady terms and bound quasi-steady omissions — large

- **Purpose/contribution:** handle `CL_alpha-dot`/`Cm_alpha-dot` separately from rotation-rate derivatives and decide whether the latter need further unsteady corrections.
- **Method/source:** begin with C's low-frequency wing-to-tail convection-delay expansion, §4.3 pp. 58-59. It supplies a leading tail-downwash-lag contribution, **not a complete unsteady wing/body solution**. A quasi-steady wake has no such history. General unsteady circulation/added-mass, frequency dependence and an appropriate validation source remain **unverified**; require a further sourced specification if those terms are material or the owner wants the full published target set (A “Unsteady flow”; C cited pages; CR A-6, p. 101).
- **Independent tests:** prescribe an analytic time-varying downwash and delay, expand it by hand, and derive tail lift and moment rate terms; zero delay removes that particular term. Independently vary pitch rotation and alpha history so a test cannot pass by conflating them. The moment/lift ratio follows the prescribed tail arm, independent of the solver.
- **Mutations:** replace delayed by instantaneous downwash, reverse delay sign, call `Cm_q` an alpha-dot derivative, apply the delay contribution twice or normalize alpha-dot as roll rate.
- **Risks / use:** a local compiled derivative does not establish the relevant frequency/method or prove omitted unsteady terms negligible. Needed if Navion alpha-dot rows are selected; it is not necessary to implement wake history for a static `CL_alpha` milestone. F-104 dynamic comparisons have the same distinction. If the complete budget cannot be supported, mark that derivative unsupported rather than declaring this leading model complete.

### T27 — Conditional propulsion/reference-state closure — large if needed

- **Purpose/contribution:** reproduce the source's actual propulsion condition, or obtain a separately documented engine-off reference. Decide whether this task is needed in T16; if it is, it blocks acceptance of dependent comparisons even though it appears after the basic airframe modules in this development order (CR pp. 100-103; TASKS H3/T10).
- **Method/source:** combine verified propeller thrust/torque/operating data with an actuator-disk inflow baseline and explicit application-point moments, then assess slipstream effects on wing/tail loads. P §§11.7.2-11.7.3 verifies momentum/energy accounting and the ideal non-swirling assumptions; it does not supply the Navion's data, finite-blade swirl, asymmetric propeller effects or detailed local slipstream field. Those need a verified extension before claiming lateral/control derivative closure. D p. 8 likewise restricts its power-effect capability to longitudinal characteristics; it cannot establish all required lateral terms.
- **Independent tests:** hand-check disk momentum/energy balances, a prescribed local-speed change's effect on a synthetic tail lift law and the thrust force/arm moment. A genuinely non-working ideal disk removes its modeled thrust/induction contribution; this is not evidence that a real stopped/windmilling propeller has no drag.
- **Mutations:** add thrust twice, omit slipstream's dynamic-pressure effect, reverse the thrust arm or equate idle throttle with engine-off.
- **Risks / use:** source state/propeller data may be unobtainable; no matching powered derivative can then be validated. Not required for a genuinely documented engine-off Navion airframe comparison, though the corresponding propeller drag/configuration still belongs in T25. Powered F-104 work needs its own jet/inlet/thrust model rather than this propeller model.

### T28 — Stage the published comparison, with traceable omissions — medium

- **Purpose/contribution:** compare the selected aircraft derivatives with independent published targets, with no new physics hidden in the comparison layer. First `CL_alpha`, then `Cm_alpha`; afterward the selected control/lateral/rate subsets, and only then any expanded drag/Mach/unsteady set.
- **Method/source:** use T16's contract, independently transcribed/checked source records when authorized, per-contribution diagnostics, mesh/perturbation/section-input sensitivity and a frozen prediction before examining a fit. Publish differences and limitations; apply an acceptance threshold only after the owner's D9 choice. Resolve propulsion dependence before accepting any affected stage (CR pp. 100-103 and appendices; TASKS H3/H4/T10; [decision sheet](../data/reference/inventory/go_no_go.md)).
- **Independent tests:** a separate hand-written comparison record with deliberately different prediction/reference units and reference origins must yield the manually derived discrepancy; mismatched variant, condition, derivative definition or missing input must produce an unavailable comparison. The analytic cases from earlier tasks remain independent anchors. A reference-tool run, if later authorized/licensed and pinned, is a secondary cross-check, not the Navion oracle.
- **Mutations:** compare a wing contribution with an aircraft total, compare different CGs, substitute a predicted value for the published target, silently zero a missing term or label a combined derivative as an individual one.
- **Risks / use:** agreement after fitting to the same target is calibration, not independent validation; correlated source errors and missing provenance remain. This milestone does not by itself satisfy queued T10's power-off performance comparisons or T11's complete aircraft requirements.

Linear subsonic PG does not supply shocks, wave drag, inlet effects or transonic stability changes. A future F-104-specific regime/source plan is separate from T16-T28; no supersonic implementation method is verified or authorized here. Multi-surface, body/interference, controls and rate modeling are common requirements, not F-104-exclusive tasks ([other-source inventory](../data/reference/inventory/other_sources.md), SL56H06 pp. 1-5, MEMO 2-2-59H pp. 2, 5-6; CR pp. 74-79; A “Compressibility”; ROADMAP v0.2).

## What the estimator can already compare, and a smaller first T10

Current synthetic verification can compare LLT elliptic-wing lift slope/loading/induced drag with the independently derived lifting-line solution; VLM circulation, lift, drag and moment with its one-panel hand solution; and force/reference, symmetry, incidence and refinement behavior within the stated idealizations. Those checks already have fixtures/tests and evidence; this documentation task did not rerun them ([LLT tests](../crates/soas-estimator/tests/lifting_line.rs), [VLM tests](../crates/soas-estimator/tests/vlm.rs), [LLT reference](../data/reference/estimator/lifting_line.md), [VLM reference](../data/reference/estimator/vlm.md)).

Do not make finite-aspect-ratio VLM equal lifting line by using the same solver output as an expected value or tightening an inter-method tolerance. The existing recorded differences, convergence and efficiency-bound checks distinguish discretization evidence from physical validity ([T15b records](../data/reference/estimator/t15b_assertions.md), [gap/efficiency discussion](../data/reference/estimator/vlm_efficiency_gap.md)). These are meaningful estimator **verification** milestones; calling them published Navion validation or completion of queued T10 would be misleading.

A smaller **published lifting-surface contribution** comparison would be meaningful if a source independently supplies that isolated component, its geometry/sections, condition and reference definitions matching the current model. The inspected Navion tables do not provide such a breakdown. **There is no legitimate wing-only versus published-Navion derivative comparison available from this inventory**, even if one derivative is wing-dominated. Subtracting a tail/body contribution predicted by our own model from the published total would not create an independent wing reference. A whole-aircraft `CL_alpha` first milestone is meaningful only after the static aircraft budget and source gates above are addressed (CR pp. 100-103; C §§2.5-2.6).
