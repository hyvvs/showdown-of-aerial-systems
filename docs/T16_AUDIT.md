# T16: Navion source and comparison-definition audit

## Scope, evidence and verdict

**NO-GO for the first whole-aircraft Navion lift-slope estimate against CR-96008.** The checked sources do not establish a single, mutually matching aircraft geometry, configuration, reference normalization and propulsion state for Teper's Navion row. Later Navion reports add useful information, including a documented propeller-removed case, but do not identify that case as the origin of Teper's row. This is an input-readiness verdict, not a verdict on the published slope or the estimator's accuracy. (CR pp. 99-103, especially Fig. X-1 and Tables X-A/B; N70 pp. 3-5, Fig. 4 p. 12; N72 pp. 6-7, 19-21; PLAN §§“T16 audit contract” and “What is already meaningful”.)

The proposed alternative is **CRM-HL / ONERA LRM-WBSHV, HLPW-5 Case 2.2**. Its public package supplies an actual airframe surface definition and matching static force curves, including horizontal and vertical tails. It has important model, reference-conversion and reuse gaps; it is a proposal for owner review, not a selected replacement or permission to model it. The same audit is applied below. (O §§6.4, 7.1.1, 8.2.3, pp. 3-4, 8; W §§III, VI.B.1, pp. 4, 17, Fig. 24 p. 20, Fig. 25 p. 21; PLAN §“Required NO-GO follow-up”.)

This deliverable is documentation only: no solver, tests, coefficient transcription or model inputs are supplied. T17 and T10 remain unstarted; the mandatory owner checkpoint after the first future comparison remains in force and no accuracy threshold is selected here. (TASKS §“T16”; PLAN opening scope, §§“T23” and “What is already meaningful”.)

Reading terms: **CG** is the center of gravity; a **datum** is the coordinate reference from which positions are measured. **Lift slope** describes how the lift coefficient changes when angle of attack changes. **Downwash** is the change in flow direction reaching the tail because of the wing. ## 1. What Teper's Navion lift slope means, and what remains unknown

### Published quantity and comparison contract

The candidate is the **CL_alpha row in Table X-B**, not a wing-section slope, normal-force slope, dimensional Z-w derivative or slope reconstructed from a transfer function. It is presented as a longitudinal derivative for the Navion aircraft. There is no component-separated wing or tail lift slope and no explicit tail-off configuration in that section. Interpreting it as an aircraft total is supported by its presentation, but the exact component/configuration history is not documented. (CR introduction pp. 1-2, pp. 99-103, A-6.)

It is also a **compiled published datum, not an authenticated Navion flight measurement**. Teper warns that accessible material can include early estimates and nominal configurations; the Navion section does not identify its originating source or method. Independence and experimental status must therefore be established rather than inferred from the NASA report number. (CR introduction p. 2, pp. 99-103.)

## 3. Go/no-go and the alternative-reference audit

The Navion verdict remains **NO-GO**, rather than GO WITH GAPS: wing/tail/body geometry and the identity/reference contract are missing inputs to the estimate itself, not merely uncertainty bars on an otherwise defined aircraft. The original plan explicitly forbids constructing these inputs from an unlabeled sketch or silently substituting another aircraft. (Geometry audit above; CR pp. 100-101; PLAN §“T16 audit contract”.)

## 5. Compressibility: Mach provenance, PG calculation and discrepancy share

The requested factor is confirmed. CR Table X-B p. 101 / PDF 107 prints **M = 0.158**; X-A/C on that page and the nominal condition on Fig. X-1 p. 100 agree. This is the source of the Mach number, not a value selected from the estimator or inferred from a generic Navion speed. (CR cited pages, visually checked; PLAN §“Current CR Navion condition”.)

The diagnostic is `F_PG = 1 / sqrt(1 - M^2)`, from A §“Compressibility”. Direct arithmetic gives **1.01272067087463**, rounding to **1.01272**; the factor is **1.272067087% above the incompressible value**. Relative to the corrected value, the uncorrected value is **1.256088795% below it**. These are derived diagnostics, not transcribed coefficients, empirical adjustments or accuracy tolerances. (A cited section; CR p. 101; PLAN §“Incompressible assessment and PG diagnostic”.)

For the proposed alternative's documented **M = 0.20**, the same calculation gives **1.02062072615966**, an increase of **2.062072616%**; the corresponding shortfall relative to the corrected value is **2.020410289%**. Both reviewed conditions are below the owner's approximate hold point, but that does not validate a whole-aircraft uniform PG scaling. The hold point remains **Mach above about 0.3 or factor increase above about 5%**, with questions at borderline conditions; these are owner review triggers, not new physical or accuracy thresholds. (O §7.1.1 p. 4; W §VI.B.1 p. 17; A §“Compressibility”; PLAN §“Incompressible assessment and PG diagnostic”.)

For a positive slope, the uniform diagnostic raises the incompressible result: it could reduce an underprediction or worsen an overprediction. It does not establish a Navion-wide correction to downwash/body interactions, and PG's linear small-disturbance assumptions do not cover arbitrary separation or high-lift behavior. (A §§“Configurations”, “Compressibility”; C §§2.5-2.6; W §VI.B.1 p. 17.)

**Its share of the expected discrepancy remains unavailable.** At the future comparison, with `a` denoting the matched whole-aircraft lift slope, report `delta_total = a_reference - a_incompressible`, `delta_PG = (F_PG - 1) * a_incompressible`, and the signed diagnostic share `delta_PG / delta_total` only when a defensible nonzero total gap exists. For an absent or near-zero gap, give the absolute diagnostic increment and its percentage scale instead. No total gap or share is invented in this audit, and this algebra is not a causal attribution or a fitting rule. (PLAN §“Share of the expected discrepancy”; A §“Compressibility”; CR p. 101.)
